# Climate Research: Growing Tomatoes in the Sausalito Fog Belt

## Understanding the Microclimate

Sausalito sits at the mouth of the Bay, directly exposed to marine air flowing in through the Golden Gate. This creates one of the most persistent summer fog environments in the Bay Area.

### Typical Summer Conditions (June–September)

| Metric | Sausalito | Ideal for Tomatoes |
|---|---|---|
| Average high (June–Aug) | 62–68°F | 70–85°F |
| Average low (June–Aug) | 52–56°F | 60–65°F |
| Morning fog frequency | 70–80% of days | Rare |
| Fog burn-off time | 11 AM – 2 PM | N/A |
| Annual rainfall | ~30 inches | 20–25 inches |
| Humidity (summer) | 75–90% mornings | 40–70% |

The key problem: tomatoes are photosynthesizing and setting fruit for only 4–6 hours per day on heavy fog days, vs. 10–12 hours in a sunny inland garden. That's 40–60% of the available solar energy.

## The Three Fog-Belt Killers

### 1. Blossom Drop
Tomato flowers drop without setting fruit when temperatures fall below 55°F at night or when daytime highs stay below 60°F for extended periods. Sausalito regularly hits both thresholds in June and July.

**What this means:** Varieties that have cold-set genetics (developed in Eastern Europe or Pacific Northwest) are significantly more likely to successfully set fruit.

### 2. Fungal Disease
Morning fog leaves foliage wet for hours each day. This is the ideal environment for:
- **Early Blight (Alternaria):** Starts on lower leaves, moves up. Weakens plant progressively.
- **Late Blight (Phytophthora):** Fast-spreading, can kill a plant in 5–7 days in wet conditions.
- **Botrytis (gray mold):** Attacks wet blossoms and green fruit — particularly devastating in fog zones.

Varieties with genetic disease resistance (especially to late blight) have a major structural advantage in Sausalito.

### 3. Insufficient Heat Accumulation
Large-fruited varieties (beefsteaks, large slicers) require 60–90 heat days to ripen a single fruit. Sausalito's typical summer provides 30–45 genuinely warm days. This is why beefsteak varieties almost universally fail in the fog belt — the math simply doesn't work.

Cherry and small-fruited varieties require far less heat per fruit and ripen in 50–65 days from transplant.

## What the Research Says About Fog-Belt Tomato Selection

### The Oregon State Breeding Program
OSU's vegetable breeding program has produced several varieties specifically engineered for cool, foggy Pacific Coast conditions. **Legend**, **Siletz**, and **Willamette** emerged from this program. They're characterized by:
- Short DTM (under 70 days)
- Cold-set capability (can set fruit at 50°F nights)
- Late blight resistance genes
- Compact, determinate growth suited to shorter seasons

### Czech and Eastern European Heirlooms
Varieties from Czechoslovakia, Russia, and the Baltic region — **Stupice**, **Glacier**, **Siberia**, **Sub-Arctic Plenty** — were developed in climates with cold summers, short growing seasons, and high humidity. They share fog-belt-relevant genetics:
- Blossom set at temperatures as low as 45–50°F
- Fast fruit development cycles
- Tolerance for overcast, low-light conditions

### Cherry Variety Advantage
The physics of small fruit favor fog-belt gardens. A 10-gram cherry tomato requires roughly 1/10th the accumulated heat of a 100-gram slicing tomato. In a Sausalito summer, this is the difference between harvesting in July and harvesting nothing at all.

## Fog Belt Best Practices for This Trial

1. **Transplant into walls of water / season extenders** — protects young plants during late-April cold snaps and extends the effective warm window

2. **Mulch heavily** — retains soil heat; soil temperature matters as much as air temperature for root development

3. **Stake and prune aggressively** — a dense canopy stays wet longer; open architecture dries faster and reduces fungal pressure

4. **Remove lower leaves proactively** — eliminate the pathway for soil splash-up that triggers early blight

5. **Morning watering only** — foggy mornings are already wet; never add evening irrigation, which leaves foliage wet overnight

6. **South and southwest-facing micros** — even a small slope or wall exposure difference can add 3–5°F, which is significant at the margin

7. **Consider black plastic mulch** — absorbs heat, raises soil temp 5–10°F, and can meaningfully extend the warm window
