## Quick Reference: What Makes a Variety Fog-Tolerant?

Three characteristics correlate strongly with fog-belt success:

1. **Short DTM (under 65 days):** Less time needed means less heat needed. Every extra week in Sausalito's fog costs real ripening potential.

2. **Cold-set ability:** Some varieties can set fruit (transition flower → green tomato) at temperatures below 55°F. Most cannot. Varieties bred in Pacific Northwest or Eastern European climates often have this.

3. **Disease resistance:** High humidity and cool temperatures invite fungal disease (early blight, late blight, botrytis). Varieties with built-in resistance (especially Oregon State breeding program) tolerate fog's damp conditions far better.

Cherry and small-fruited varieties also have a built-in advantage: less accumulated heat is needed per fruit vs. a large beefsteak.
